Volunteering isn't just something I do — it's a big part of who I am, shaped by years in Scouting and my time supporting veterans in my community.

Read My Story

Veterans Village

I've volunteered with Veterans Village, an organization dedicated to helping veterans get back on their feet after service through housing support, job resources, and a strong sense of community. Working alongside veterans and hearing their stories gave me a deep appreciation for the sacrifices they've made and reinforced how much of a difference a little time and effort can make in someone's life.

Eagle Scout

Earning the rank of Eagle Scout, the highest honor in the Boy Scouts of America, was one of the most meaningful achievements of my life. For my Eagle Scout project, I led the ground-up build of a new playroom at the Ronald McDonald House near Rady Children's Hospital in San Diego, working alongside the San Diego Padres from May through August 2019. I designed and built the furniture myself — including a two-level octagonal bookshelf — and fundraised and collected books to fill it, giving kids and their families a space to decompress during some of their hardest days. Reaching that rank took years of commitment, leadership, and service, and the project taught me discipline, responsibility, and how to lead a team toward a shared goal. That foundation is a big part of why service continues to matter to me today.

Trail Rebuilding

From 2015 through 2022, I worked on trail rebuilding as part of Boy Scouts — a way to get outside in nature, help out the people who'd use the trails after me, and stay connected with my troop when COVID made it impossible to meet indoors.

Why It Matters

Giving back keeps me grounded. It's easy to get caught up in school and career goals, but volunteering reminds me that success means more when it's used to lift up other people. Whether it's veterans, my community, or future projects I take on, I try to carry that mindset with me in everything I do.
